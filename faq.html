Shell Cordovan Care & Inquiries

Essential guidelines for conserving, polishing, and commissioning heirloom Shell Cordovan leather clutches and accessories.

Why does Shell Cordovan ripple rather than crease like calfskin?
Standard leather consists of corium and grain layers with differing densities that buckle and crease under flexure. Shell Cordovan is purely the uniform, high-density subcutaneous shell layer. Without grain separation, the leather rolls into smooth, graceful waves rather than sharp cracking creases.
How should I treat white bloom or wax spots on my clutch?
White wax bloom is a definitive mark of authentic Shell Cordovan. It occurs when rich currying fats migrate to the cooler surface during storage. Simply buff the surface briskly with a pure horsehair brush; frictional heat will liquefy the waxes and drive them back into the fiber matrix, restoring a brilliant mirror shine.
What should I do if my Cordovan clutch gets caught in rain?
Allow the clutch to dry naturally at room temperature away from artificial heat sources. Water may cause microscopic surface fibers to swell temporarily, creating pale spots. Once dry, smooth the surface with a glazed porcelain burnisher or genuine deer bone, followed by vigorous horsehair brushing.
Why do your clutches use two-needle saddle stitching instead of sewing machines?
A sewing machine lockstitch uses two interlocking threads that unravel rapidly if one strand breaks. The traditional French saddle stitch passes two needles simultaneously through the same hole; even if a stitch is severed by friction, the opposing loop remains tightly cinched and the seam never pulls apart.
What is the production lead time for a bespoke commissioned clutch?
Because each clutch is hand-cut, hand-beveled, and assembled through sixty hours of meticulous artisan labor, bespoke commissions require twelve to sixteen weeks from leather selection to final inspection and delivery.
